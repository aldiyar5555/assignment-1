Alan Turing

1912–1954

Alan Turing was a British mathematician, logician, and computer scientist whose groundbreaking work changed the way we understand computation, intelligence, and modern technology.

Biography Timeline

  • 1936 — Published his influential paper “On Computable Numbers,” introducing the concept of the Turing machine.
  • 1939–1945 — Worked at Bletchley Park to help break the German Enigma code during World War II.
  • 1945 — Helped design the Automatic Computing Engine, one of the earliest ideas for a programmable digital computer.
  • 1950 — Proposed the Turing Test in his paper “Computing Machinery and Intelligence.”

Key Achievements

  • Formalized the foundations of computation and algorithms.
  • Made tremendous contributions to cryptanalysis and wartime intelligence.
  • Laid the groundwork for artificial intelligence research.
  • Shaped modern ideas about what machines can and cannot compute.
“We can only see a short distance ahead, but we can see plenty there that needs to be done.”

— Alan Turing

Legacy

Turing’s ideas remain central to computer science, mathematics, and AI. He is remembered not only as a genius inventor but also as a visionary who connected logic, machines, and human thought.
